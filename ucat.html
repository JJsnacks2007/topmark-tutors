How it works

Time goes where you actually lose it.

Time in this course is spent where students actually lose marks and time, not split evenly across the four sections. An even split feels fair and it is the reason most preparation plateaus, because the section costing you the most is getting the same hour as the section you already clear comfortably.

Every practice test comes back marked with a breakdown by cause, so the next week's work is decided by that breakdown rather than by whichever section you feel like doing.

In every session

  • Timed practice every class
  • Marked tests with a cause breakdown
  • Live teaching, not recordings only

What is covered

Four sections. Four different ways to run out of time.

The UCAT ANZ test has four subtests, sat in this order. Question counts and timings below are the official ones published by UCAT ANZ.

44 Q / 22 min

Verbal Reasoning

Roughly 30 seconds a question, so the time goes on reading the passage twice instead of going to the question first and reading only for what it asks.

35 Q / 37 min

Decision Making

The most generous section per question, and the easiest to overspend in: full diagrams get drawn for syllogisms and Venns that a partial one would have settled.

36 Q / 26 min

Quantitative Reasoning

Time goes on arithmetic done by hand when the on-screen calculator, or an estimate against the options, would have closed the question faster.

69 Q / 26 min

Situational Judgement

Under 23 seconds a question, and the time goes on answering as yourself rather than against the published guidance for medical and dental students.

Section list, question counts and timings verified against the official UCAT ANZ test format page on 14 October 2026. Abstract Reasoning is no longer part of the test. Re-check before publishing, as UCAT ANZ can change the format between cycles.

Questions

UCAT Online Course FAQs

Who is this for?

Students sitting the UCAT for medicine or dentistry entry, typically in Year 12 or a gap year, and Year 11 students starting early. You do not need to have started preparing: the lecture classes teach the content from the beginning.

Is it online only?

Yes. Every session is live online, so you can join from anywhere in Australia or New Zealand. There is no in-person option for this course.

What if I miss a session?

Nothing to arrange. Classes are paid for one at a time, so if you cannot make one, you do not pay for it. There is no catch-up fee and no penalty, and you simply come to the next one.

Are sessions recorded?

No. Sessions are live and are not recorded. Teaching to a camera is not the same as teaching a room that can interrupt you, and the workshop classes only work because everyone in them is actually working.

When should I sit the UCAT?

That is your call, and it should be. The useful rule is to sit it at a time that does not collide with school: pick a window with no assessments due, so the fortnight before the test belongs to the test. Check the current booking and testing dates on the official UCAT ANZ site, since they move each year.

What is the refund policy?

The first class is free, so you can see the whole thing before paying for anything. After that there are no refunds. Because classes are paid individually, the most you are ever committed to is the next class.

How do I pay?

By bank transfer, the same as the Crash Course. We send an invoice once payment is received. Classes are priced individually, $35 for a lecture class and $45 for a workshop class, or $75 a week for both, so you only pay for what you take.

How is this different from buying a question bank?

A question bank gives you more questions. This course gives you feedback on the ones you have already done: every practice test comes back marked with a breakdown by cause, so you find out whether a lost mark was timing, method or reading rather than only that it was lost.
